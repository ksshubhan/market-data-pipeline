Why the 19 Sep suite cannot see a wake

  Blocks 2 and 3 start a consumer and push or close on the next line.
  The consumer has to start, spin 8192 iterations and take the lock
  before that, or it never parks, and in 200 runs it never did. A
  deleted notify_one is only observable if the consumer is already
  blocked in wait() when the push or close happens. The suite has to
  establish that, not hope for it.


How the new blocks establish it

  wait_nonempty() increments parks_ under the mutex and calls
  not_empty_.wait(lock, pred) in the same critical section. parks()
  takes the same mutex. So a test thread can only read parks() >= 1
  after the consumer has released the lock, and after the increment
  the only place it releases the lock is inside wait(), which releases
  and blocks as one atomic step. A spurious wakeup reacquires the lock,
  finds the predicate false and waits again. From the moment the test
  thread reads parks() >= 1, the consumer is in wait() until something
  notifies it. That is a property of the locking, not of timing.

  Margin:   park/wake measures ~1.3 us and the consumer's spin ~10 us.

            absorb scheduler preemption on a loaded laptop. The
            evidence that it is enough is the unmutated suite passing
            N of N below.

Stop condition

  Carried from the 19 Sep Fallback line and not relaxed: M5 and M6
  each exit 1 with their predicted FAIL line on 100 of 100 runs. Two
  preconditions make that result interpretable: the unmutated suite
  exits 0 on 100 of 100, which shows the deadlines produce no false
  FAIL, and M9 exits 0 on 100 of 100, which shows the new blocks test
  that a wake happens and not how often notify_one is called.

  If any M5 or M6 run exits 0, the stop condition is not met and the
  row stays open with the count recorded. The one known way that can
  happen is a spurious wakeup after the push, which finds data and
  returns; a spurious wakeup after close() finds closed_ set and
  returns the same way. That possibility is why N of N is a claim
  about this machine and not a proof. It is not grounds to lower the
  bar after the run.
